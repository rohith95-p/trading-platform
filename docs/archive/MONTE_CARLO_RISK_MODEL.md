# Monte Carlo Risk Model & Input Distribution Specification

## 1. Executive Summary

This document specifies the quantitative assumptions, sampling methodology, and statistical properties of the Monte Carlo simulation suite in Ultra Core (`scripts/validation/part1_suite.py` and `scripts/validation/monte_carlo_ruin.py`). It serves as the official, empirical replacement for superseded coarse estimates.

---

## 2. Methodology & Input Distribution Assumptions

Unlike parametric models (e.g., Gaussian, Student's t) that fail to capture fat tails, positive skew from 1:3 payoff structures, and real-world execution friction, Ultra Core uses **empirical non-parametric bootstrap resampling with replacement**.

### Key Model Assumptions
1. **Trade Distribution Source:**
   - Empirical trade P&L sequence $X = \{x_1, x_2, \dots, x_N\}$ generated by the validated 4-leg Portfolio V5 (`14b5467a4f969b25`) on the out-of-sample holdout window (2025-01-01 to 2026-05-20).
   - Sample size $N = 2,319$ trades.
   - Cost scenario: Realistic ECN execution ($0.25 spread, slippage model, round-trip commissions).
2. **Resampling Process:**
   - Uniform random sampling with replacement from $X$ using a 64-bit Mersenne Twister/PCG64 PRNG (`numpy.random.default_rng(11)`).
   - Trajectory length: Exactly $N = 2,319$ trades per path.
   - Total simulated paths: $M = 10,000$ independent trajectories.
3. **Capital & Account Constraints:**
   - Starting balance: $S_0 = \$100.00$.
   - Sizing: Base 0.01 lots per order (governed by `SizingLadder`).
   - Drawdown evaluation: Continuous peak-to-trough path monitoring.
4. **Failure Thresholds:**
   - **Terminal Ruin:** Equity drops to $\le \$20.00$ (80% account drawdown).
   - **Margin Call / Trading Floor:** Equity drops to $\le \$50.00$ (breaching the $50 SizingLadder floor).
   - **Quarter Drawdown:** Equity touches $\le \$75.00$ (25% account drawdown).

## 3. Empirical Results (10,000 Simulations on 2,319 OOS Trades)

Validated under `research/validation/part1_montecarlo.json`:

| Metric | Monte Carlo Value | Target / Assessment |
|:---|:---:|:---|
| **Simulated Trajectories** | 10,000 | Statistically robust ($SE \approx 0.27\%$) |
| **Median Profit Factor ($P_{50}$)** | **1.299** | Strong center of distribution |
| **5th Percentile PF ($P_5$)** | **1.161** | Bottom 5% remains profitable (> 1.0) |
| **95th Percentile PF ($P_{95}$)** | **1.455** | Upper bound under favorable sequencing |
| **Probability of Ruin ($P(\text{Equity} \le \$20)$)** | **8.15%** | Vastly reduced from legacy 48.6% (15% risk) |
| **Probability of Hitting Floor ($P(\text{Equity} \le \$50)$)** | **20.05%** | Small accounts face sequence risk without circuit breakers |
| **Probability of -25% Drawdown** | **43.16%** | Common pullback over 2,300+ trades |
| **95th Percentile Max Drawdown** | **97.28%** | Worst 5% of sequences exhaust unconstrained small accounts |

## 4. Operational Implications & Capital Requirements

1. **Why Circuit Breakers are Mandatory:**
   Because 8.15% of unconstrained trade sequences touch $20, live trading **must enforce** the cascading daily 6% loss breaker (`DAILY_LOSS_LIMIT_PCT = 0.06`) and consecutive loss pausing in `src/core/risk_rules.py` (`ENFORCE = True`).
2. **Minimum Account Size:**
   While mathematically survivable at $100 with 0.01 lots (median PF 1.299), account safety increases dramatically above $300-$500 where 0.01 lot stop distance ($1.75-$2.50) represents <0.5% of equity.
