# Audit, 2026-11-01: the data and the engine, and what the numbers really are

The owner asked for the weak parts of this project to be taken as far as they can
go. Four of the ten were about the same thing — where the bars come from and
whether the engine is honest about them — so they were done together. This is
what changed and what it cost.

No strategy, stop, target, session or lot size was changed.

---

## 1. The engine will no longer resolve an exit on a 15-minute candle

Exits are found inside each 15-minute candle on 1-minute sub-bars. Where minute
data was missing, the engine quietly used the 15-minute candle itself, where a
stop and a target are both "touched" and which came first is a guess. That single
substitution is the largest known source of inflated results here: the same
portfolio reads PF 1.38 on coarse candles and PF 0.93 on minute bars.

`EngineConfig.m1_policy` now has three values and defaults to the strict one:

| value | behaviour |
|---|---|
| `require` *(default)* | raise `MissingM1Error` at the first candle with no minute data |
| `skip` | never substitute: leave the candle unsimulated, count it, and count any open position that passed through it unchecked |
| `coarse` | the old fallback, for reproducing old runs only |

The three October screening scripts that deliberately ran on coarse candles now
say so in code, and carry a note that their DEV/TEST figures are not comparable
with minute-bar results.

**What the strict run says about every number in this repository:**

| window | candles with no minute data | exits left unchecked | candles only partly covered |
|---|---|---|---|
| DEV 2022-08 → 2024-06 | 4 | **0** | 761 (1,204 minutes) |
| TEST 2024-06 → 2026-01 | 2,130 | **0** | 402 (1,000 minutes) |
| RECENT 2026-07 → 2026-10 | 0 | **0** | 102 (199 minutes) |

Zero exits were resolved without minute data, in any window. The 2,130 empty
candles in TEST are the known September–October 2025 hole, and because an entry
cannot fill without minute prices either, nothing trades inside it.

## 2. The third-party archive was trusted seven weeks too long

Minute bars before mid-2026 come from a 22-year Kaggle archive, not the broker.
Nothing had ever measured whether it agrees with the broker's own feed. It does,
until it doesn't. Over the 81,232 fifteen-minute candles both cover:

| year | median difference | 95th | more than $1 apart |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2022 | $0.058 | $0.121 | 0.00% |
| 2023 | $0.051 | $0.119 | 0.01% |
| 2024 | $0.052 | $0.168 | 0.15% |
| 2025 | $0.047 | $0.196 | 0.12% |
| **2026-01** | **$0.526** | **$2.46** | **28.8%** |
| **2026-02** | $0.113 | **$16.60** | 12.5% |

Through 2025 the archive is the broker's feed to within about five cents. From
January 2026 it is a different series. The boundary was set at 2026-02-18, so
roughly seven weeks of disagreeing prices sat inside the TEST window.
`KAGGLE_END` is now **2026-01-01**, and TEST ends with it.

`scripts/validation/data_check.py` re-measures all of this on demand.

## 3. One master series instead of three data folders

`research/data`, `research/data_2026-10-30` and `research/data_live_parity`, plus
the archive, were merged by hand inside research scripts — `merged_bars()` in one
study, a different `bars()` in the audit runner. Two studies could test two
different histories with nothing to show it.

`src/backtesting/master.py` now declares every source, which span each may
supply, and which clock it is on, and `load_master()` is the only loader research
code calls. The broker's own feed wins wherever it has bars; the archive only
fills in behind it.

A consequence worth saying out loud: before mid-2026 the 15-minute candles are
the broker's own while the minute bars underneath them come from the archive.
That split is real and it is why `scripts/data_refresh.py` exists.

## 4. The broker-native history can now grow

The terminal only ever hands out its newest ~100,000 bars, which at one minute is
about fourteen weeks. That is the whole reason the archive is needed at all, and
no single download can fix it. `scripts/data_refresh.py` appends each pull into
`research/data/broker_archive/`, never truncating, so a weekly run grows the
bot's own minute history a week at a time until the archive is no longer needed.

## 5. What the corrected numbers are

Run through the clean pipeline, strict on minute data, live 4-leg portfolio,
0.02 lots, realistic costs, Atlas guard on:

| window | trades | net | PF | win | worst drawdown |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| DEV 2022-08 → 2023-05 | 132 | **−$295** | 0.66 | 28.0% | $306 |
| TEST 2024-06 → 2025-11 | 253 | **+$1,058** | 1.40 | 42.3% | $239 |
| RECENT 2026-07 → 2026-10 | 54 | **+$790** | 1.81 | 50.0% | $194 |

**DEV stops in May 2023 because the Atlas guard stops it.** The drawdown reaches
$306, crosses 60% of the $500 trailing limit, and new entries are blocked from
then on. That is the guard working: on 2022–23 data this account would have been
frozen nine months in. With the guard off, DEV runs the full 22 months and ends
at **−$210 over 337 trades**.

This is the most important line in the audit, and it is worse than what was
reported before: DEV was previously quoted at PF 1.07, from a one-off script that
no longer exists and whose settings cannot be reconstructed. The number above
comes from a committed, re-runnable pipeline.

The honest reading: **this portfolio earns in trending gold and bleeds in quiet
gold.** 2022–23 was range-bound and it lost. 2024–26 trended and it won. A flat
stretch is the expected bad case, not a surprise — and the Atlas evaluation does
not have to survive a bad stretch, it has to reach +$150 before −$500, which the
Monte Carlo puts at about 74%.

## 6. The broker is now measured, not assumed

Six things about a broker change how this bot trades, and all six were constants
tuned to one Exness demo account: the gold symbol's name, its price decimals (the
spread gate is in points, so `MAX_SPREAD_POINTS = 350` means $0.35 at three
decimals and $3.50 at two), the server's clock (every session gate compares
`bar_time % 86400`, which is server time), the contract terms, the swap, and what
the account allows.

`src/core/broker_probe.py` reads all of it at startup, writes
`logs/broker_profile.json`, compares it with `config/broker_expected.json`,
rescales the spread gate to keep its dollar meaning, and sends a WARN alert
naming each difference. The server clock is measured from a live tick and
reported as unknown rather than guessed when the market is shut, because a tick
that is a whole number of hours stale looks exactly like a different timezone.

This is what closes most of the Atlas readiness gap mechanically: when the Atlas
login exists, the bot says what is different instead of trading on the old
assumptions.

## 7. The holdout is enforced, not just promised

The rule was that data from 2026-11-01 onward may judge a decision and never
choose one. `load_master()` now cuts those bars off unless a caller passes
`allow_holdout=True`, so a script cannot reach them by accident, and
`scripts/validation/prereg_check.py` (wired into CI) checks that every recorded
result has a pre-registration committed before it.

Three of the existing results had their pre-registration committed in the same
commit as the result, which proves nothing about the order. They are reported as
weak rather than quietly passed, and from now on a pre-registration gets its own
commit before the run.

## 8. Code

`main_loop.py` 1,434 → 1,159 lines. The Atlas bridge (saved state, account
translation, breach wording) moved to `src/core/atlas_bridge.py`; the status
snapshot, ops heartbeat and foreign-exposure alert moved to
`src/core/live_status.py`; the trailing-stop and pyramiding code, disabled since
2026-09-03 and measured net-negative, is gone along with the virtual-stop file it
maintained. Every name those callers used still resolves on `main_loop`.

The `.agents` rules were rewritten: they still described `portfolio_v4`, 0.01
lots and a $105 account.
